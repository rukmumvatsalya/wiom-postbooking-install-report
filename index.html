Read as: June → July, 8–30 Jun vs 1–22 Jul 2026. Figures are for customers who booked in each window.

Install-waiters are patient while things keep moving. The moment a promised slot is breached, frustration triples and roughly a quarter try to cancel — and that failure is growing.

This report covers only genuine install-waiters — customers who booked and are on their way to a technician. Existing customers who re-booked (about 7% of the queue, plus the address-pending and post-install states they cluster in) are excluded, because a real install-waiter has no connection yet and cannot have an “internet not working” problem.

On-track customers are calm — frustration 9.0% (June) and 9.4% (July) — asking where the technician is, rescheduling, and calling the field team. But customers whose slot was breached run 32.2% → 27.5% frustrated and 22.1% → 22.6% ask to cancel. Slot breaches grew from 458 to 665 customers — a growing failure, and the single highest-leverage place to intervene.

The cliff is at arrival. Half of bookings get a technician assigned (50% June, 51% July), but only ~29% see the technician arrive — a ~21-point drop at the single most important step. Install lands at 25% (June) / 26% (July) of bookings. This is the hard-data twin of the slot-breach anger in the chat: the technician not arriving is both the biggest funnel leak and the top cause of frustration.

Two cautions before reading these ratios

First, app-install→booked is a period ratio, not a tracked cohort — the app-analytics id does not join to the booking mobile, so this is bookings-in-window over installs-in-window, not ‘these exact 48,118 people.’ Second, the installed row is right-censored: a customer who booked on 22 July has had only days to get installed, so July’s install count is understated and the two months’ install rates are not yet comparable. June, with a month of runway, is the more complete read at ~25% of bookings installed.

Most failed installs are non-serviceable areas

The single biggest reason a booking never becomes a connection is that the area cannot actually be served (55–57% of non-installs). We are taking bookings — and booking fees — in places no partner can install. Scheduling latency, customer abandonment, and CSP (partner) non-response follow. The slot-breach frustration in the chat is real, but it is a downstream symptom: many of those breached slots were never serviceable to begin with.

Speed, when it works, is fine: median booking-to-install held at 1.3 days (June) and 1.2 days (July); same-day installs 33% → 35%, within two days ~65% in both. The problem is not how fast installs happen — it is how few happen at all.

G and H are broken

Variants G (9.4%) and H (12.4%) install at a third of the rate of I, I1, C, D and E (31–35%). H alone carries 1,726 bookings at 12% install — a large volume of booking fees taken against an experience that rarely ends in a connection. Worth pairing with the pre-booking variant analysis to see whether these variants also mislead on price or serviceability.

Three-quarters who chat come back

~87–89% of fee-payers contact support, and of those, roughly three-quarters do so more than once — on average 3–4 separate conversations (median 2). Only about a quarter of fee-payers chat once and are done. This is the demand signature of a stalled install: the same customer returning to ask ‘where is my technician?’ again and again. Every repeat contact is avoidable if the install simply happens on time.

Read with careWhat changed since v1, and the limits.

Review notes & limits

  • Genuine install-waiters only. Existing customers who re-booked are fully excluded: a naive read of all install-labelled states put frustration at ~18%, but that pooled in customers chatting about their current broken service (they can't have an ‘internet not working’ issue if they aren't installed yet). Removing them — by intent (internetIssue, password, refund) and by language — drops frustration to ~12% overall and leaves the real install queue: calm on-track, angry when breached.
  • No contact-rate denominator yet. These are customers who chatted, not everyone in each state. The population per stage can come from MESSAGE_HISTORY outbound workflows once the identity key is resolved (post-booking id is MOBILE/ACCOUNT_ID, not USER_ID) — that would turn “33% frustrated” into “X% of everyone whose slot we breached.”
  • Why existing customers sit in install states is unconfirmed — most likely lapsed customers re-booking, whose account shows a new booking while their old connection is live and broken. That existing-customer load is real and worth its own report; it is simply not this report, which is the install queue.
  • Frustration is the model’s sentiment label, and the bot’s reply is only partly recorded, so this measures what customers said, not whether they were helped. Small states (under ~120 customers) are marked thin.
  • Real phone numbers exist in the source; quotes are limited to phrases used by 2+ customers and scrubbed of numbers, names and addresses. Internal use only. Two windows, 23 vs 22 days — per-customer rates compare, raw totals do not.
