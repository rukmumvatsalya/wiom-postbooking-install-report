What booked customers ask while they wait to be installed

Every support message from a customer between placing the order and going live — read carefully, because two very different groups are hiding in the same states, and only one of them is actually waiting for an install.

Chatted from install states  7,328 → 8,113 incl. existing customers …genuinely awaiting install  2,842 → 4,194 Messages  64,410 Peak frustration  33% on slot breach

While things keep moving, customers wait patiently. The moment a promised slot is breached, a third turn angry and nearly a quarter try to cancel — and that failure is growing.

Split the “booking→installation” chat by what customers are actually there for and it separates cleanly. The 6,530 customers genuinely making their way to a technician are calm — only 10.6% of their messages are frustrated — they ask where the technician is, reschedule, and call the field team. But the 1,293 customers whose slot was breached run 32.8% frustrated and 23.0% ask to cancel. Slot breaches grew from 540 to 801 customers between the two windows (+48%), and cancel-on-breach rose 20.9% → 23.2%.

A caution that reshapes the naive numbers: the single largest “installation” state, address-pending, is 54% existing customers chatting about their current broken service, not the install. Its frustration is 85.9% among “my internet isn’t working” messages versus just 3.7% among genuine install questions. Read the install story from the technician states, not the raw totals.

Two cautions before reading these ratios

First, app-install→booked is a period ratio, not a tracked cohort — the app-analytics id does not join to the booking mobile, so this is bookings-in-window over installs-in-window, not ‘these exact 48,118 people.’ Also, the paid-booking-fee figures count all fee payers in the window (the same base the contact-rate section uses), not only those with a booking-confirmed event in the window — so that sub-line is not strictly nested under the booked row. Second, the installed row is right-censored: a customer who booked on 22 July has had only days to get installed, so July’s install count is understated and the two months’ install rates are not yet comparable. June, with a month of runway, is the more complete read at ~27% of bookings installed.

How to read this table

The top row is the real installation queue — large, patient, low anger. The second is the failure mode: when a booked slot is breached, frustration triples and cancellation jumps. Per window, only 2842→4194 of the 7,328→8,113 customers are genuinely awaiting install; the rest sit in install-labelled states but are mostly existing customers. The bottom two rows are mostly not install-waiters at all — freshly-installed or re-booking customers whose net is down, which is why a quarter of their messages are “internet not working” and almost none reschedule or ask about a technician.

Review notes & limits

  • This is a corrected read. An earlier draft treated all 18 states as one install funnel and reported ~18% frustration as install anger. Interrogating it showed the biggest state is 54% existing customers — frustration there is 85.9% among broken-service messages vs 3.7% among install questions. The tiers above fix that misattribution.
  • No contact-rate denominator yet. These are customers who chatted, not everyone in each state. The population per stage can come from MESSAGE_HISTORY outbound workflows once the identity key is resolved (post-booking id is MOBILE/ACCOUNT_ID, not USER_ID) — that would turn “33% frustrated” into “X% of everyone whose slot we breached.”
  • Why existing customers sit in install states is unconfirmed — most likely lapsed customers re-booking, whose account shows a new booking while their old connection is live and broken. Worth confirming with the state-machine owners; it is both a data-labeling issue and a real support signal.
  • Frustration is the model’s sentiment label, and the bot’s reply is only partly recorded, so this measures what customers said, not whether they were helped. Small states (under ~120 customers) are marked thin.
  • Real phone numbers exist in the source; quotes are limited to phrases used by 2+ customers and scrubbed of numbers, names and addresses. Internal use only. Two windows, 23 vs 22 days — per-customer rates compare, raw totals do not.
